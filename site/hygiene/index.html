How this is computed

Every hour we compare each published ASPA with the upstreams we see in BGP. In an AS path, the AS to the left of a network received the route directly from it; we call that an observed upstream. The paths come from this portal's route-collector feeds (RIPE RIS and RouteViews; paths with AS_SETs, loops or private ASNs are skipped). CAIDA's AS-relationship inference tells us whether each upstream is a provider, a peer or a customer. A provider we observe that the ASPA does not list is shown as "observed on <date> via AS Y, not in ASPA".

Evidence. A hop seen by at least 2/3 of the feeds is strong, by at least 1/4 partial, by fewer weak. Weak hops may be backup or regional links that most of the Internet never sees, and cannot be confirmed from collector data.

Caveats. We only see paths that reach our collectors. CAIDA's labels are inferred and can be wrong; for example, large domestic networks that peer may appear as providers. Each hop therefore shows its share of the network's routes and how many feeds saw it. Extra providers in an ASPA (listed but not seen) are allowed and usually mean backup links. This page describes routing data, not intent.

Registry and country: the NRO combined delegation statistics. ASPAs: this project's validated RPKI data (rpki-aspa-data). AS relationships: The CAIDA AS Relationships Dataset (CAIDA Acceptable Use Agreement). Computed by aspa-validation.
